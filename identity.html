Brand & visual identity

The look and feel of ByteForge.

A reference for how we show up — logo, color, type, imagery and voice — so the brand feels consistent from a product label to a repair invoice.

Our story

Started in a repair shop, not a boardroom.

ByteForge opened in 2014 as a two-person repair bench fixing laptops for university students in Boa Vista. Word spread because we explained what was actually wrong instead of upselling parts nobody needed. Today we sell hardware too, but the bench mentality never left — plain-language advice, fair pricing, real warranties.

Our identity reflects that: precise, technical, and calm — electric blue for confidence, deep ink for reliability, circuit-inspired geometry throughout.

Logo

The mark combines a rounded circuit chip with a "B" monogram — the anvil-like form of the letter nods to "Forge," built in circuit board geometry.

ByteForge
Do

Keep clear space around the mark equal to the corner radius of the chip. Use approved color combinations only.

Don't

Don't stretch, rotate, recolor outside the palette, or place the mark on a busy photo without a solid backing.

Imagery style

Clean, technical, well-lit.

Product photography is shot on a neutral background with even lighting — components in focus, cables tidy, nothing staged to look more "lifestyle" than it is. Repair photography shows real hands, real tools, real workbenches.

Voice & tone

Plain-language, never condescending.

We explain hardware the way we'd explain it to a friend — no jargon dumps, no fear-based upselling. If a customer doesn't need a part, we say so, even when it costs us the sale.

  • Lead with the fix, not the upsell.
  • Use real specs, not marketing adjectives.
  • Short sentences. One idea at a time.
